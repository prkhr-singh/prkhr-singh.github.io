# Experiments with AI

I tend to understand a new technology by giving it a real problem. These are a few cases where I used AI to make sense of decisions I was actually facing—not demos built in search of a use.

The original tools contain personal information and keep changing as the decisions change. So this is a portfolio of what became possible, how I approached it, and what I learnt along the way.

## Featured experiments

### [Home Buying Command Center]({{ '/ai/home-buying-command-center/' | relative_url }})
I had to compare property types, suburbs, contracts, valuations, inspections, personal taste and finances—often all at once. I built one working system to hold the research and still help me make a decision.

### [Waurnvale Interiors]({{ '/ai/waurnvale-interiors/' | relative_url }})
I knew when I liked a sofa or a rug in a shop. I had almost no idea whether they would like each other—or my house. This became a way to test colours, proportions and complete rooms before buying anything.

### [Wheel or Wire: Total Cost of Ownership]({{ '/ai/wheel-or-wire-tco/' | relative_url }})
A car's price is obvious. Its cost is not. I built an interactive model for comparing used versus new, petrol versus EV, finance offers, running costs, resale and the opportunity cost of taking money out of a home-loan offset account.

## Earlier projects and notes

## [Abbotsford Sublease Landing Page]({{ '/abbotsford-sublease.html' | relative_url }})
A rapid Airbnb-style listing generated with AI prompts, then tuned with real photos, pricing, and context to market our Abbotsford apartment sublease.

How a coding assistant and an LLM help me build and maintain a minimal Jekyll site: small, auditable changes; simple CSS; and GitHub Pages for deployment.

## [The Digital Self — An AI Autobiography]({{ '/ai/the-digital-self/' | relative_url }})
An AI‑generated autobiography from my tweet export (5,384 tweets, 2011–2025). A creative reconstruction exploring how narrative and “voice” emerge from a long public feed.
