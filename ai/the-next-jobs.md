Most of the conversation about AI and work is about the jobs it will end. That list is already fairly well mapped. The more interesting question, and the harder one, is which jobs it will start.

I wanted a better answer than optimism or alarm, so I went looking for evidence. The best evidence available is history: two earlier technological revolutions that also arrived with predictions of mass unemployment, and that also ended with more work than they began with—slowly, unevenly, and rarely for the people who were displaced.

## What I made

A 100-slide perspective deck in six parts:

- **The industrial age**, from handloom weavers to horses: what was lost, why work grew anyway, and where the new jobs appeared;
- **The computer and internet age**, from spreadsheets and ATMs to e-commerce and platforms;
- **Seven rules** that held across both, such as: an automated occupation grows only if cheaper output unlocks more demand, and value moves to whatever stays scarce;
- **The evidence so far** on AI, tested against those rules;
- **Glimpses of the next economy** in AI-native firms and the new categories of company forming around them; and
- **Thirteen predictions** for 2026 to 2040, each with a confidence level and a test that would prove it wrong.

## Where AI helped

I used AI as a research partner and an editor: to pull sources together, check claims against them, pressure-test the rules against counter-examples and keep a long argument consistent from the first slide to the last. The judgement calls, especially in the predictions, are mine.

There is a small irony in that. The deck argues that the scarce work will be verification, accountability and context—exactly the parts of the job that stayed with me while making it.

## What changed for me

The history made me less worried about whether new work will appear and more concerned with who it reaches and how long it takes. This time the early cost is falling on people entering the workforce rather than those already in it. For someone in a strategy and operations role, the practical conclusion is to move towards the bottleneck: the judgement, sign-off and coordination that AI makes more valuable rather than less.
