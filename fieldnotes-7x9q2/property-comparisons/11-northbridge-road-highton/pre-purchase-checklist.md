## Part 1: The Private Sale Process — What You Need to Know

### 1.1 How a Victorian Private Sale Actually Works

This is not an auction. You have more time, more protection, and more leverage than most buyers realise.

**Step-by-step:**

1. **You express interest** (verbal, to Tom). This is non-binding and costs nothing. It opens a conversation.

2. **You receive and review the contract.** Tom emails the Contract of Sale + Section 32 (Vendor Statement). Do NOT sign anything before your conveyancer has reviewed the S32. Typically 1–2 business days for a conveyancer review.

3. **You make a written offer.** In VIC, an offer is made by signing the Contract of Sale with your offer price and any special conditions. A holding deposit (usually $1,000–$5,000) is paid to the agent's trust account at this point — this is not the full deposit, just a signal of good faith. It is fully refundable if you cool off.

4. **The agent presents your offer to the vendor (the owners).** The agent is legally obliged to present all written offers. The agent works for the vendor, not you.

5. **Vendor accepts, counter-offers, or rejects.** Counter-offers are common. This is done verbally through the agent, then formalised when both parties sign.

6. **Both parties sign the same contract.** The property is legally sold at the moment both signed copies are exchanged. You receive a copy. The 3-business-day cooling-off clock starts the day after you sign.

7. **Cooling-off period (3 business days).** During this window:
   - Commission your building and pest inspection
   - Have the conveyancer complete the full S32 review
   - Verify heating/cooling
   - Ask any remaining questions
   - You can exit by giving written notice to Tom by midnight on Day 3. Penalty: 0.2% of purchase price (~$1,540 at $770k). This is your insurance policy.

8. **Cooling-off expires → you are committed.** Once the window closes without notice, you are contractually bound. The deposit (typically 10% for established property) is due at this point or on a date specified in the contract.

9. **Settlement (usually 30–60 days later).** Conveyancer manages the title transfer, mortgage drawdown, and final payment.

**Key protection facts:**

| Protection | Detail |
|---|---|
| Cooling-off period | 3 clear business days (days start the day AFTER you sign) |
| Cost to exit during cooling-off | 0.2% of purchase price = ~$1,540 at $770k |
| Can you add conditions to the offer? | Yes — finance approval, building inspection, subject-to-S32 review. Conditions create more time but may weaken the offer vs a clean unconditional. |
| Deposit before cooling-off expires? | Only a small holding deposit ($1–5k). The full 10% is typically not due until settlement or sometimes when cooling-off expires. Confirm the specific schedule in the contract. |
| Can the agent accept a better offer during cooling-off? | Technically yes — you are not fully "under contract" until the cooling-off window closes if you signed conditionally. If you sign unconditionally and both parties sign, the property is sold. |
| Can the vendor cancel? | Not after both parties sign. The binding moment is mutual execution. |

### 1.2 The Negotiation Mechanics — What You Can and Cannot Do

**What you can control:**
- The offer price
- Settlement date (Tom said the owners want 30–60 days — align to what suits you for liquidity)
- Conditions in the contract (finance approval, B&P result, S32 review)
- Whether you include a "subject to pre-approval" clause (weakens offer but protects you)
- The holding deposit amount (higher = more serious signal to vendor)

**What you cannot control:**
- Other offers — the agent must tell you if a competing offer arrives, but not the price
- The vendor's timeline for responding — no obligation to respond within any period unless you put an expiry on your offer
- The agent's framing of your offer to the vendor

**The existing $760k offer:** Tom disclosed this proactively. This is unusual and may signal he wants genuine competition to help justify rejecting the $760k offer to his vendors. Use this information — it confirms $770k is competitive without being premium. Do not be drawn into bidding significantly above $780k on the basis of a disclosed competing offer you cannot verify.

**Offer expiry tactic:** When you make your written offer, include a clause: "This offer is open for acceptance until 5pm [2 business days from offer date]." This prevents the agent from sitting on your offer while fishing for higher bids. It creates urgency without weakening your position.

**Conditional vs unconditional:** 
- A clean unconditional offer at $770k signals confidence and allows the vendor to move quickly — they are more likely to accept at a slightly lower price vs waiting for a higher conditional offer.
- A conditional offer ("subject to finance" or "subject to satisfactory B&P") gives you more protection but can be rejected in favour of a cleaner unconditional.
- **Recommended approach:** Rely on the cooling-off period rather than written conditions. Make the offer close to unconditional (if pre-approval is in hand), knowing you have 3 days to walk for $1,540. This is the equivalent of a $1,540 insurance policy.

### 1.3 Multiple Offers — What the Rules Are

If another buyer is also making an offer, the agent must inform you. The agent cannot disclose the exact price of the competing offer, but can confirm a competing offer exists. In a multiple-offer situation:
- You are typically asked to submit your "best and final" offer by a deadline
- The vendor can choose any offer — not necessarily the highest
- Settlement terms (your flexibility, deposit size, conditions) can be the differentiator
- A higher holding deposit signals commitment

### 2.4 The Deck — Condition and Compliance [COOLING-OFF]

The deck is a key lifestyle asset. But on a sloped block, it requires specific checks:

- **Permit:** Was a building permit obtained for the deck? This will appear in the S32 (permits and certificates of final inspection). An unpermitted deck creates an insurance liability and can be a problem at resale.
- **Height above ground:** If the deck edge is more than 1m above natural ground, it likely required engineering sign-off for the balustrade. Check the permit confirms this.
- **Material condition:** Composite decking vs timber. Composite is lower maintenance; timber on an elevated deck requires annual sealing.
- **Wind exposure:** A deck on an elevated Highton block can be exposed to southerly winds. On a cold winter Saturday, if the deck is too exposed to use comfortably, the "outdoor lifestyle asset" becomes a visual rather than a usable one.

## Part 3: Legal and Planning — What the Conveyancer Must Check

### 3.1 Section 32 — The Critical Review Items [CONVEYANCER]

The Section 32 (Vendor Statement) is the vendor's legal disclosure document. Your conveyancer's job is to read this forensically. Here is what they must specifically look for and report back to you:

**Title and encumbrances:**
- [ ] Is the title clear (no mortgages, caveats, or charges outstanding)?
- [ ] Are there any easements? What type — drainage, power, right of carriageway, access? Where do they run? (A drainage easement through the backyard is common and usually fine; a right-of-carriageway for a neighbour is significant.)
- [ ] Is there a covenant on the title? **Specifically: does covenant AJ739795K (confirmed on No. 10 Northbridge) also apply to No. 11?** Your conveyancer needs to check both the title certificate and the Covenant Register for this specific instrument number.

**Zoning and overlays:**
- [ ] What is the planning zone? (Expected: GRZ — General Residential Zone)
- [ ] Are there any overlays? Design and Development Overlays (DDO), Significant Landscape Overlays (SLO), Heritage Overlays (HO), Bushfire Management Overlay (BMO), Land Subject to Inundation Overlay (LSIO)?
- [ ] **Flood:** The Barwon River is in the Highton area. Is there an LSIO (Land Subject to Inundation Overlay) affecting this specific block? The property is on an elevated site (Northbridge is a ridge road), which significantly reduces flood risk, but the conveyancer must confirm the absence of an LSIO. The S32 must include a planning certificate that discloses this.
- [ ] **Bushfire:** Is there a Bushfire Management Overlay (BMO) on this title? Highton is a suburban area with limited bushfire risk relative to the Otways, but elevated treed blocks in the Barrabool Hills corridor can attract BMO controls. The S32 planning certificate will disclose this.

**Building permits and certificates:**
- [ ] Are permits for the home's construction, deck, garage, and any extensions on file?
- [ ] Have certificates of final inspection been issued for all permitted works?
- [ ] Is there an owner-builder certificate of consent? (If the home was built by an owner-builder, different warranty obligations apply.)
- [ ] Are there any current planning enforcement notices or outstanding building orders?

**Services:**
- [ ] Is the property connected to town water and sewerage (Barwon Water)?
- [ ] Is it connected to gas? (Gas is preferred for heating; if not connected, gas heating requires a new connection which can cost $2,000–$5,000 for the supply line alone.)
- [ ] Electricity — any solar export agreement, or embedded network?
- [ ] What does the S32 say about telephone/NBN? (The report.md flags this as an item to verify.)

**Rates and outgoings:**
- [ ] What are the annual council rates? (Approximate: $1,500–$2,200/year for a Highton house at this price point)
- [ ] What is the Barwon Water annual bill?
- [ ] Is there a GAIC (Growth Areas Infrastructure Contribution) levy? (Not expected for Highton, which is established — this applies to greenfield growth areas.)
- [ ] Is there any Land Tax liability? (Applies if the vendor owns multiple properties; the vendor's Land Tax clearance must appear in the S32.)

**Owners Corporation (Body Corporate):**
- [ ] Is there any Owners Corporation affecting this title? (A freestanding house on its own title normally has none. Confirm there is no common driveway, common infrastructure, or plan of subdivision creating OC obligations.)

### 3.2 Covenant AJ739795K — What It Might Say [CONVEYANCER]

This covenant was confirmed on 10 Northbridge's title in the Codex research. Covenants on Highton properties from the 1990s–2000s typically restrict:
- Minimum house quality (e.g., no fibro or weatherboard cladding)
- Single dwelling per lot (no subdivision or second dwelling without removing covenant)
- Building envelope controls (setbacks, height)
- Outbuilding materials or size
- Keeping animals

A covenant restricting materials from a 1990s developer perspective might technically conflict with black metal cladding, but in practice most such covenants have never been enforced and have low-standing risk. Your conveyancer will:
1. Obtain a copy of the covenant instrument from the Land Registry
2. Read the exact wording
3. Advise whether any existing features of the home (the black cladding itself being the obvious test) comply or were built in potential breach
4. Advise the practical enforceability risk (most residential covenants are rarely enforced unless a neighbour is aggrieved)

**The critical question:** If the black cladding was built in breach of a materials covenant, does that create a problem for you as a buyer? In practice — almost certainly no. The breach was committed by a prior owner, the cladding exists and is visible, and no one has challenged it. But your conveyancer should formally advise on this.
