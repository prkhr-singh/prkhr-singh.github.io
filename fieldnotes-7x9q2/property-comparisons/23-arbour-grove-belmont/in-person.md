## First Impression

New, clean, and three-bathroomed. But the interior immediately felt optimised for occupancy count rather than livability. Small windows created a noticeably darker interior than expected from photos. The townhouse design sacrificed warmth and character for unit-count efficiency. Within minutes, the emotional verdict was clear: "zyada hi practical optimization" (too practically optimised). It did not feel like a home.

## Room-by-Room Notes

**Master bedroom:** Identified during walkthrough ("ye hai master"). One master-associated area felt somewhat compact. No specific size complaint but the room did not feel generous.

**Ensuite:** Present. Newer (2024 build). Compact — consistent with AI expectation of tight ensuite for this footprint.

**Walk-in / built-in robe:** Storage noted as "kaafi hai" (sufficient) during initial scan. However, the absence of WIR for a property at this price point was noted — particularly when compared to established homes in the same range.

**Bedroom 2 / Bedroom 3:** Rooms carved out for maximum occupancy. Four rooms arranged so multiple people could share bathrooms — rational developer logic, but not how a family lives. Made the property feel like shared accommodation rather than a family home.

**Main living / lounge:** Felt compressed despite being "spacious" in floorplan. Layout did not create a natural, flowing living area.

**Kitchen:** Adequate per initial impression. Storage described as usable despite smaller footprint. Good efficiency within constraints.

**Laundry:** Present. Noted but not detailed.

**Outdoor / alfresco:** Not specifically detailed but outdoor space was noted as a positive — the AI pre-assessed this as strong (9/10) and this was not contradicted in person.

**Garage / parking:** Garage present and noted as practical value near end of walkthrough.

**Other — bathrooms:** THREE bathrooms confirmed. Agent confirmed: "this has three bathrooms." Repeated several times — this is the property's single standout feature for the price point. One, two, three counted on walkthrough. Lower-level bathroom described as "decent size."

## What AI Got Wrong / Missed

**Massively overestimated overall fit (70/100 vs in-person 38/100):** The AI assessed this as a reasonable 3-bed townhouse with strong bathroom count and manageable OC risk. In person, the property failed on the dimensions that matter most: character, light, and the "would you actually want to live here" test.

**Missed — interior darkness:** Small windows were immediately noticeable and created a significantly darker interior than comparable properties. This is inherent to the townhouse design and cannot be fixed without structural changes.

**Missed — "zyada hi practical" feel:** The AI can score rooms and storage but cannot assess whether a home feels emotionally flat. This one did. Both visitors noticed. The design prioritised units-per-floor over livability.

**Missed — townhouse format vs hard criteria:** The hard criterion ("freestanding house only, no shared driveways, no body corporate") was pre-flagged in criteria.md. The OC risk is real (flagged by AI) but the broader issue — that this is effectively a townhouse in character despite technical classification — was more damning in person than on paper.

**Missed — grant eligibility uncertainty:** Built 2024, agent believed previously occupied but was not certain. This affects FHOG ($20k regional Geelong) and potentially stamp duty concession. If occupied: both grants disappear. AI assumed favorable grant status without verification.

**Overestimated — resale appeal:** A 4-room developer-pattern townhouse surrounded by identical units (investor-dominant nearby stock, lease listing noticed nearby) will have moderate resale depth. The "lots of same-style homes" observation reduces the uniqueness premium in resale.
