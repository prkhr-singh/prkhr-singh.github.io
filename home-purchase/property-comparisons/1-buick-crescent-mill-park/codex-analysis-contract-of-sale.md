## Legal And Title Watchpoints

### 1. Building and pest conditions are not active for auction

The contract page says "Loan - NOT APPLICABLE AT AUCTION", and the building report and pest report boxes are not checked. That means you should assume an auction purchase is unconditional unless your conveyancer negotiates something different in writing before signing.

Action: do independent building and pest before auction, not after.

### 2. Strong purchaser-risk special conditions

The special conditions are vendor-protective. They say the purchaser accepts the property based on their own inspection and enquiries, accepts defects latent or patent, accepts dilapidation/infestation, and cannot object or claim compensation for building compliance, defects, service issues, encroachments, restrictions, planning scheme matters, contamination, or similar issues.

This is not unusual in Victorian contracts, but it matters here because the property includes recent owner-builder outdoor works and water authority build-over conditions.

Action: send the special conditions to your conveyancer and ask whether any amendment is possible before auction. At minimum, understand that the contract is trying to push most physical/building risk onto you.

### 3. Easement and sewer/water asset conditions

The title plan shows an easement along the rear part of Lot 2, marked E-1. The plan describes E-1 as drainage and sewerage / sewerage. Yarra Valley Water says it has imposed conditions on structures on or near water/sewer assets and/or easements, and those conditions bind successors in title under section 148 of the Water Act 1989.

The Yarra Valley Water material says, in substance:

- structures such as verandahs, pergolas, gazebos and decking near sewer assets can be allowed only subject to clearance and foundation conditions;
- some structures can be built over sewer mains with minimum 600mm horizontal/vertical clearance;
- structures cannot be built over sewer branches servicing adjoining property or multiple lots;
- maximum fill and vertical clearance limits apply;
- YVW can require access and the conditions bind future owners.

Action: ask your conveyancer to check whether the existing deck/verandah/alfresco and any outdoor kitchen/heater structures comply with the YVW build-over approvals and whether any part blocks future access. Ask the building inspector to physically assess this, not only the contract.

### 4. Owner-builder verandah and decking works

Council certificate identifies Building Permit BS-U 41708 / 3447125423203 for "Verandah & Deck", permit date 11 June 2020, final/occupancy date 14 October 2020.

The attached owner-builder report dated 18 April 2026 covers the rear verandah and decking works only, not the whole house. It found:

- no serious safety hazards observed;
- no major defects observed;
- very few minor defects observed;
- exposed/weathered timbers need painting/sealing;
- overall condition average;
- undetected defect risk moderate due to obstructions/access limits;
- no roof exterior inspection due to height restrictions;
- according to owner, the louver along the boundary fence was an add-on after permit completion;
- further advice from verandah designer and/or building surveyor is recommended for the louver's design/construction compliance;
- structural engineering, electrical, plumbing and council compliance inspections are recommended.

This is the biggest practical watchpoint. The report is reassuring in that it did not find major defects in the owner-builder works, but it is limited and explicitly recommends further checks.

Action: commission an independent building inspector and ask them to focus on the deck/verandah, supports, posts, footings, roof flashing, drainage, timber weathering, fasteners, compliance of the louver/privacy screen, outdoor heater/electrical/plumbing, and whether loads are appropriate.

### Yarra Valley Water Build-Over Rules

There are two Yarra Valley Water build-over applications in the S32 material:

- Application ID 408193, dated 17 July 2019: proposed development could not proceed as submitted.
- Application ID 430372, dated 18 December 2019: proposed development may proceed subject to conditions.

The later December 2019 approval is the one that appears to have enabled the eventual 2020 verandah/deck works, but your conveyancer should confirm this.

The practical construction rules disclosed in the YVW material include:

- lightweight structures such as garages, carports, pergolas, verandahs, gazebos, sails and decking can be built over a sewer main only subject to conditions;
- 600mm horizontal and vertical clearance is required around sewer mains;
- structures cannot be built over a property connection branch servicing an adjoining property or multiple lots;
- 600mm horizontal clearance is required from that branch;
- driven piles are not permitted;
- pad footings/foundations are permitted;
- where pad footings are not structurally acceptable, foundations must extend to the angle of repose to the sewer invert level;
- any structure over a YVW sewer asset must not exceed 8m in length;
- if a structure traverses the sewer main, foundations crossing the sewer main need 600mm vertical clearance unless demountable;
- concrete floors must be non-structural infill slabs if removal would otherwise affect structural integrity;
- 24-hour unobstructed access is required;
- eaves are limited to 600mm maximum width;
- a structurally independent roof is required when attached to another structure, with engineering plans showing independence;
- below-ground ponds are not permitted;
- rockeries and garden ornamentation are permitted only if easily relocatable;
- YVW may enter the land and excavate through owner works to inspect, maintain, repair or replace assets, and YVW will not reinstate the owner works.

### Can You Enclose The Alfresco Sides?

Based on the contract alone, you should assume "not without checking and probably applying again".

The YVW consent says any variation to the owner's application/plans or owner works requires a new application to Yarra Valley Water, which may be approved or rejected. It also limits certain structures over/near sewer assets and requires access, clearances, roof independence and demountability/relocatability in some cases.

So, enclosing the two open sides could be possible only if the enclosure:

- does not breach the sewer main/branch clearance rules;
- does not make the structure non-accessible or non-demountable where access is required;
- does not add structural loads in a way that affects the sewer asset;
- complies with building/planning rules;
- is approved by YVW if it varies the existing approved works;
- is approved/certified by council/private building surveyor if the enclosure triggers a permit.

Before buying, ask the agent/vendor for the approved 2020 plans and the final YVW-approved design. After buying, do not enclose it casually with fixed walls/glazing until a building designer/surveyor and YVW have confirmed whether a fresh application/permit is needed.
