The engineer's risk assessment says:

Recommendations:

**Interpretation:** the engineer gives a genuine de-escalation of the subsidence concern. The floor-level problem is real, but not described as active structural failure.

The unresolved limitation is important: the report could not independently verify footing as-builts or founding depth. It relies on available drawings, photos, visible condition, and assumptions that mandatory inspections were completed.

## The Actual Issue Cluster

The recurring pattern across the documents is not "the whole house is failing." It is more specific:

1. The house has a localized floor-level issue.
2. The engineer says this is likely serviceability-related, not structural failure.
3. The surrounding site has drainage and moisture-management weaknesses.
4. The retaining wall/front deck area shows movement or instability.
5. Some external ground/water conditions may be feeding the broader movement risk.
6. The property has no evident termite management system, and moisture/drainage conditions increase future pest risk.

In plain terms: **the house itself may be structurally okay, but the land/water/deck/retaining-wall environment around it needs active correction.**

## Decision View

### I would not walk away automatically

The engineer report reduces the biggest fear. If the only issue were localized floor-level variation with a $10.9k underpinning quote and no structural distress, the property could still be a rational buy at the right price.

This is not a clean, low-maintenance, polished home in the full due-diligence sense. It presents well, but the reports show it needs active building management.

The correct price posture is: **discount for known defects and unknowns.**

## Recommended Position

I would keep it alive only under these terms:

1. **Do not go unconditional** without conveyancer review and explicit comfort on reliance.
2. **Get an independent builder/engineer or at least a phone call with Adams** to confirm what "serviceability-related" means in practical buyer terms.
3. **Price in more than $10.9k.** The underpinning quote is not a total repair budget.
4. **Treat drainage as mandatory, not optional.** If drainage remains wrong, the floor/deck/retaining-wall problem can recur.
5. **Ask for a deck/retaining-wall quote.** This is the largest unpriced risk.
6. **Use the reports to negotiate.** The defects are documented and not speculative.
